# Blink — the 2:30 script

Lines in **bold** are spoken to Blink. Everything else is what to watch for.

## Prep, before you hit record

1. **Put the day job on Google Calendar**, 06:00–18:00, one single event per
   weekday (not a recurring series), then sync. Without this, "six to six" is a
   sentence Blink cannot act on: there is no tool that stores a working pattern
   from speech, and it will happily book your video prep at 16:00.
2. Open the web app once so the timezone re-syncs. The workspace is empty and
   the profile survived the reset, but confirm the clock reads Lusaka time.
3. Google Calendar open in a second tab, week view.

---

## 1 · 0:00–0:35 · The brain dump

> **"I need to prep and record a hackathon demo video, it's due tomorrow night
> and needs about four hours. My day job takes six to six on weekdays, so work
> around that. Plan it."**

Two session cards appear, stacked. Verified live: this phrasing returns two
sessions rather than one, which is the shot you want.

Say nothing over the cards landing.

---

## 2 · 0:35–1:05 · The rest of the picture

Blink asks what's next. Answer it:

> **"There's more. A client project for the next three weeks, a marketing site,
> an admin dashboard and a mobile app. And I want to finally learn music
> production, I've got an Akai MPK Mini in a drawer collecting dust. None of
> it's planned."**

Let it capture that. Do not let it finish planning the lot.

---

## 3 · 1:05–1:45 · The interruption, and the search

Cut in while it is still asking:

> **"Hold on. Tomorrow's a public holiday here, the government announced it
> yesterday. Can you check that, and factor it in?"**

Blink asks permission to search, naming the query. Say **"yes"** on camera.

The grounded answer lands with real sources: 1 September 2026, declared for the
presidential inauguration. **This is the beat where a search changes the plan
rather than decorating it.** Let the sources sit on screen.

---

## 4 · 1:45–2:10 · It uses the day

> **"So I'm free all day tomorrow. Put the client project in there, a few
> sessions across the day at times that are actually open."**

It reads the freed day and places several sessions on the one task. Watch the
Google Calendar tab.

---

## 5 · 2:10–2:30 · Close, to camera

Not to Blink. Straight to the lens, roughly:

> "What you just saw was one conversation. No forms, no dragging blocks around.
> It planned the urgent thing first, checked a fact it did not know against the
> real web, and used what it found to change a real calendar.
>
> Under it, Gemini decides what to do and ordinary code decides what is true, so
> it can't tell you it did something it didn't. It runs on Cloud Run, it keeps
> working after the conversation ends, and it's live right now.
>
> Thanks for watching."

---

## If a beat misbehaves

- **Beat 1 returns "couldn't place them yet"** — you dropped the four hours or
  the "plan it". Both are load-bearing. Re-say the line as written.
- **Beat 3 finds nothing** — re-run the search once; if it still comes back
  empty, say so on camera and move on. Blink refusing to confirm what it cannot
  verify is the honesty rule working, and narrating over it is the one thing
  that would actually cost you.
- **Beat 4 places nothing** — the day job event for tomorrow is still on the
  calendar. Delete tomorrow's, re-sync, retry.
